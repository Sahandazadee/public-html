Hardware tools

Breadboard and jumper wires

Build circuits without soldering. Two 830-hole breadboards (the ESP32 board is wide; lesson 1.4) plus male-to-male and male-to-female jumper wires.

EssentialFrom lesson 1.4

Multimeter

Your eyes in the world of electricity: it measures voltage, current, resistance and broken wires (continuity/beep mode). A simple digital multimeter is enough.

Very importantLessons 1.4, 6.1 and troubleshooting

Image: Arduino Docs, CC BY-SA 4.0

Soldering kit

A pencil soldering iron with temperature control (around 300 to 350 °C), 0.8 mm rosin-core solder, an iron stand, and a sponge or brass wool. You'll need it to solder header pins onto modules.

OptionalLesson 1.4

Image: Arduino Docs, CC BY-SA 4.0

Basic parts

LEDs in a few colors, 220 Ω and 10 kΩ resistors, push buttons, a 10 kΩ potentiometer, an LDR and a passive buzzer.

EssentialFrom lesson 1.4

Modules

DHT22, SSD1306 OLED display, SG90 servo, microSD card module, NEO-6M GPS module, relay module, ESP32-CAM with the ESP32-CAM-MB base.

Chapter 3 onward

🔌 A USB "data" cable

Many cheap cables only charge and have no data wires. If your computer doesn't see the board, swap the cable first.

Essential

Exact shopping list (for the whole course)

Buy in three stages. Each stage is complete for the lessons named in its heading: with stage 1 you can do every lesson up to 3.1, with stages 1 and 2 everything up to 4.3, and with all three the whole course. Rows tagged Optional are only for side sections, examples or extra comfort; you can follow every lesson without them. Before you buy, read lesson 1.2 (choosing a board); once the parts arrive, start with lesson 1.4 (hands-on bench). No parts yet? Most circuits also run in the free Wokwi simulator (lesson 2.3).

Where to buy

Any electronics store works: DigiKey and Mouser (genuine parts, fast shipping in North America and Europe), Amazon, AliExpress (cheapest, but often weeks of shipping), or a local electronics or maker shop. An "ESP32 starter kit" or "Arduino sensor kit" often contains most of the stage 1 parts, and many of stage 2 (servo, buzzer, DHT22, OLED, motor, relay). Compare the kit's contents list with these tables and buy only what's missing.

A word from your coach about buying

The cheapest part isn't always the cheapest choice. A bad USB cable or a loose breadboard can cost you hours of troubleshooting. Buy from a seller who takes back faulty parts, and keep a few spares of the parts you use most (LEDs, resistors, jumper wires). Burning out a part is a normal part of learning.

AI tools

Fundamentals first, AI second

AI will suggest a wrong pin, an outdated library or dangerous 5 V wiring with complete confidence. You can only use it well once you can judge its answers. That's why AI comes in the last chapter (lesson 6.2).

Claude

Explaining code, finding bugs, writing code with explanations. Claude Code can work directly in your project folder.

ChatGPT and Custom GPTs

General questions and answers, plus GPTs built for Arduino and the ESP32. Always check the output with the checklist from lesson 6.2.

Wokwi AI assistant

Helps you build the circuit and code right inside the simulator, so you can test the result immediately.

GitHub Copilot

Code autocompletion inside VS Code, alongside PlatformIO.
