⚡ USB-C Dual Function — Charge + Program

One USB-C port handles both charging and programming.
VBUS (5V) → TP4056 → LiPo charging  |  D+ / D− → CH340C → ESP8266 UART  |  CC1/CC2 → 5.1kΩ pull-down (USB-C spec required)
Auto-reset: DTR→100nF→RST  +  RTS→100nF→GPIO0
Power path: USB 5V + LiPo → dual Schottky diode OR → HT7833 (programs even with dead battery)

Espressif's flagship low-cost WiFi SoC. The -12F variant has a PCB antenna and 4 MB flash soldered on. Castellated pads allow direct SMD soldering onto your PCB — far smaller footprint than the D1 Mini dev board (22×16 vs 34×26 mm). Boot strapping: GPIO0 HIGH = normal boot; GPIO0 LOW = flash mode. Always keep GPIO15 pulled LOW and GPIO2 pulled HIGH.

WCH's popular USB-to-UART bridge. The CH340C (SOP-16) or CH340N (SOT23-6, tinier) have a built-in crystal — no external oscillator needed. DTR and RTS lines connect via 100 nF capacitors to ESP8266 RST and GPIO0 for one-click Arduino IDE upload (auto-reset). Works on 3.3 V directly.

Linear LiPo charger with constant-current / constant-voltage (CC/CV) profile. Charge current set by a single resistor on PROG pin. CHRG pin goes LOW (LED on) during charging; STDBY goes LOW when full. Input comes directly from USB-C VBUS (5 V). No external MOSFETs needed.

Ultra-low quiescent current LDO — ideal for battery-powered devices. Input fed by LiPo (3.7–4.2 V) or USB 5 V via Schottky diode. Powers ESP8266-12F, CH340C, OLED, and sensors. Dual Schottky diode power-OR allows programming even with a dead battery (USB 5V → diode → HT7833 IN).

USB-C SMD Connector

Charge input + programming port
Size~9 × 7 × 3.5 mm
StandardUSB 2.0 type-C
Currentup to 3 A
CC pins5.1 kΩ pull-down each

MountSMD (4 pads + shell)
VBUS → TP4056 IN (charging) D+ → CH340C D+ (programming) D− → CH340C D− (programming) CC1/CC2 → 5.1kΩ → GND (required!)
USB-C receptacle replaces micro-USB. CC1 and CC2 must have 5.1 kΩ pull-downs to GND — without them, USB-C chargers cannot identify the device and may not supply current. For USB 2.0 (data-only, no PD), this is all that's needed. VBUS powers charging; D+/D- carry serial data to CH340C.

The HC-SR04P ("P" = power-compatible) works at 3.3 V and outputs 3.3 V logic on the Echo pin — no voltage divider or level shifter required. Distance = (Echo pulse width × speed of sound) / 2. Transducers protrude through lid holes (Ø 16 mm).
